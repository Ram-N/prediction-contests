## How the contest was won

![Top finishers stacked by round]({{ site.baseurl }}/active-contest/data/plots/wrapup/wrapup_top15_stacked.png){: .img-fluid }

A few things stand out in this chart:

**Subbu's strength was everywhere.** He didn't dominate any single round outright — but he never had a weak one either. 25 in the Group Stage, 26 in R32, 24 in R16, and a perfect-tier 28 in ST-421. That's what an overall win looks like: consistent excellence, no disasters.

**The R16 was where Tees made their move.** 28/32 in the Round of 16 — tied for the best score in that round — pushed Tees into medal contention from mid-pack. The contrast with the ST-421 (only 16/32) shows a participant who peaked at the right moment but couldn't sustain it through the knockout picks.

**bala varadarajan and Subbu were the ST-421 kings**, both scoring 28/32 in that round. Bala's 96 total is a commanding second place — but he needed the Group Stage (22) to be stronger to catch Subbu.

**Ranga Setlur won the R32 outright** with 30/32 — the highest single-round score in any category — but couldn't translate that into a top-3 overall finish. The other rounds simply weren't there.

## The LR-421: The Hardest Contest

The Long-Range 4-2-1 contest asked participants to predict the 4 semifinalists, 2 finalists, and the winner — **before the tournament began**. 55 people entered, and most of them got burned.

The difficulty: picking **England and Argentina** as semis while the crowd was piling onto Brazil and Portugal. And picking **Argentina in the Final** when most people assumed it would be France or Spain meeting each other.

Only two participants solved it completely.

![LR-421 final scores]({{ site.baseurl }}/active-contest/data/plots/wrapup/wrapup_lr421_scores.png){: .img-fluid }

**Guru Bhat** and **Shaj** both scored a perfect **12/12** — the only two out of 55 participants to get all 7 predictions right. Four correct semifinalists, the right two finalists (Spain and Argentina), and Spain as champion. Both had Argentina in their semis and finals when most of the field was writing off South America outside Brazil.

The next group (11/12) includes Kunal Soni, Manish Bhatt, Subbu, Tees, and Vishal Goyal — all of whom picked Spain to win and had 3 of the 4 semis correct, but missed exactly one.

The median human score was **9 out of 12**. Out of 55 participants, only 7 scored 11 or 12. This was the contest that most separated real foresight from popular opinion.

## How the AIs Did

Three AI models competed across all scoring rounds: ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

![AI vs. humans across all rounds]({{ site.baseurl }}/active-contest/data/plots/wrapup/wrapup_ai_comparison.png){: .img-fluid }

### The headline numbers

| Model | GS | R32 | R16 | ST-421 | LR-421 | Overall |
|-------|:--:|:---:|:---:|:------:|:------:|--------:|
| ChatGPT 🤖 | 24 | 24 | 24 | 20 | 9 | **92** |
| Claude 🤖  | 20 | 28 | 24 | 20 | 10 | **92** |
| Gemini 🤖  | 21 | 28 | 16 | 20 | 10 | **85** |
| *Human best* | *27* | *30* | *28* | *28* | *12* | *103* |

**ChatGPT and Claude both finished with 92 points — tied for 4th place overall**, ahead of around 30 human participants. That's a strong result by any measure. Gemini at 85 landed around 13th.

### Where the AIs were strong

**The Round of 32 was an AI showcase.** Claude and Gemini both scored **28/32** — tied for 2nd in the entire field, just two points behind Ranga Setlur's contest-best 30. In a round defined by correctly calling clean favourites in 16 straight matches, the AI models were nearly flawless. They got 14 of 16 right.

**The LR-421 is where the AIs showed their read on Spain.** All three AI models correctly predicted Spain as the World Cup winner *before the tournament began*. At a time when Spain was popular but not a universal pick (45% of humans had them winning), the AI consensus was unanimous and correct. Claude and Gemini also got all four semifinalists right, scoring 10/12. ChatGPT missed England as a semi-finalist (picked Brazil instead) and scored 9/12.

### Where the AIs fell short

**The ST-421 is where the AIs couldn't compete.** All three scored just 20/32 — eight points behind the leaders. The gap came down to the Final: the AIs backed France as their winner pick, which was the crowd consensus (61% of human ST-421 participants) and also wrong. The three humans who scored 28 correctly predicted Spain in the Final *despite France being the heavy favourite*. That's the kind of contrarian tournament read that AI models, trained to track consensus, consistently struggle to make.

**Group Stage was mixed.** Scores ranged from 20 (Claude) to 24 (ChatGPT) against a human top of 27. The Group Stage rewards getting 3rd-place teams right within groups — the kind of fine-grained edge-case knowledge where local expertise sometimes beats pattern-matching.

**Gemini's R16 (16/32)** was the lowest score of any AI or top-10 human in that round. A string of upsets in the Round of 16 hit Gemini harder than the other models.

### Strengths vs. weaknesses — a summary

| | Strength | Weakness |
|-|---------|---------|
| **ChatGPT** | Consistent across rounds (no catastrophic miss) | Missed England as an LR-421 semi; ST-421 Final call |
| **Claude** | Best R32 performance (28/32), correct Spain LR-421 winner | Weak Group Stage (20/32); missed Spain in ST-421 Final |
| **Gemini** | Correct Spain winner in LR-421 | R16 performance (16/32); same ST-421 Final miss |
| **All 3 AIs** | Predicting clear favourites in knockout rounds | Picking contrarian tournament winners against crowd consensus |

The pattern is consistent with what we've seen in other contests: AI models are excellent at straightforward probability — "who beats whom in this match" — but struggle to back minority consensus calls even when evidence supports them. France at 61% human pick was the safe call. Spain was the right one.
