## Reasoning

**Key factors influencing these predictions:**

1. **Group stage form and momentum.** Group 1 is a brutal draw — all four teams topped their respective first-round groups and are unbeaten. India won all four matches including a 61-run demolition of Pakistan. South Africa went 4-0 with Aiden Markram's 86* against New Zealand showing their batting depth. West Indies were explosive (Shepherd's 5/20 hat-trick, 30-run win over England). Zimbabwe are the tournament's Cinderella story — unbeaten with wins over Australia and Sri Lanka, powered by Brian Bennett (unbeaten all tournament) and Muzarabani (9 wickets, best bowling figures by a Zimbabwean at a T20 WC).

2. **Home advantage and conditions.** Group 1 plays in India. India's spinners (Varun Chakravarthy leading the tournament wicket charts, Kuldeep Yadav) are devastating on home tracks. Group 2 plays in Sri Lanka, where spin also dominates — giving Sri Lanka co-host advantage and potentially neutralizing Pakistan's batting power.

3. **Squad depth and bowling quality.** India's depth is unmatched — Bumrah hasn't even been fully stretched yet. South Africa have Ngidi (4 wickets vs Canada), Markram, Miller, and Stubbs providing batting firepower. Sri Lanka lost Matheesa Pathirana to injury, capping their ceiling at the death, but their spin attack led by Theekshana remains lethal on home pitches.

4. **Betting market alignment.** India as tournament favorites (~11/8) and South Africa (6/1) aligns with my Group 1 pick. Markets have India and South Africa as the strongest pair. My Group 2 pick diverges slightly — most markets favor England and New Zealand or Pakistan, but I'm backing Sri Lanka's home conditions and spin advantage over New Zealand's inconsistent middle order (Phillips, Ravindra, Mitchell all struggled outside one innings each). England's batting depth under Harry Brook gives them the edge for the top spot.

5. **Why I differ on Group 2.** West Indies at 33/1 pre-tournament and now surging would be the contrarian call for Group 1, but I think India and South Africa's all-round quality prevails. In Group 2, the market may be overrating Pakistan (inconsistent group stage, needing the last match to qualify) and New Zealand (middle-order fragility exposed). Sri Lanka's tournament NRR of +3.125 was the best in the competition, and Nissanka's form (199 runs, including a 52-ball century vs Australia) makes them dangerous at home.

**Downside risks:**

*Group 1:* West Indies' power-hitting (Rutherford, Shepherd) could upset South Africa in a single match, and Zimbabwe's Muzarabani-Evans pace duo has proven capable of dismantling any lineup. The margin between 2nd, 3rd, and 4th is razor-thin. India losing even one match could create a three-way NRR scramble.

*Group 2:* Pakistan's spin attack (mentioned as a key challenge for NZ) could peak at the right time — they dominated Namibia by 102 runs to qualify. England lost to West Indies by 30 runs in the group stage, suggesting vulnerability against quality pace. Sri Lanka without Pathirana may leak runs at the death in crunch moments.
